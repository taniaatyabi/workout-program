Strength & Mobility

A plan for someone who sits at a desk all day, hasn't trained in a while and wants to feel loose and strong rather than wrecked. Machines and dumbbells first. Barbells once your form is boringly consistent.

Why it looks like this

Long desk days shorten the front of your hips and chest, and quietly switch off your glutes and the muscles between your shoulder blades. That pattern sits behind a lot of the stiffness you're feeling. Most of this plan goes into the back of your body and the deep core that keeps your ribs stacked over your pelvis.

Strength work is the main event, and it's progressive: you'll lift a little more, or for a little longer, almost every week. The cardio is deliberately easy. Incline walking, steady rowing and the cross trainer build your fitness and help your joints recover without eating into your strength sessions.

Cardio goes after the weights on purpose. You want fresh legs and full attention for the lifting, and an easy 20 minutes afterwards is a good way to cool off.

Three blocks of four weeks

Weeks 1–4 Learn the moves 2 × 12

Rest 60–90 seconds between sets. Pick weights you could lift about 15 times. Clean reps and turning up three times a week are the whole goal here, nothing heroic. Cardio 15 minutes.

Weeks 5–8 Add a set 3 × 10

Rest 90 seconds. The glute bridge turns into a hip thrust against a bench with a dumbbell on your hips. Cardio 20 minutes.

Weeks 9–12 Go heavier 3 × 8

Rest up to 2 minutes. Swap the goblet squat for a barbell box squat in the squat rack with the safeties set. Move to barbell RDLs if your dumbbell ones look tidy. Cardio 20–25 minutes.

Week 13 is an easy week at about two thirds of your weights. After that, run weeks 9–12 again starting a step heavier.

When to add weight

Use the two-for-two rule. When you hit the top reps on every set with clean form, two sessions in a row, go up by the smallest jump available: 1–2 kg on dumbbells, one plate on a machine. Your reps drop a little, then you build them back up.

Log every session in your phone notes. Without a record you'll guess, and guessing usually means lifting the same weight for months.

How hard it should feel

About 7 out of 10. Finish each set feeling you had two or three good reps left. Lower every rep for a slow count of three.

Grinding out one last ugly rep is exactly where the shaking kicks in, and it teaches your body nothing useful.

Picking a starting weight

Go lighter than your pride wants on day one and do a practice set of 12. Felt like a 5 out of 10? Go up a step. Form broke? Go down. Rough starting points for a lot of women new to lifting: 4–6 kg dumbbells for presses, 8–10 kg for RDLs and goblet squats. Your practice set decides, not this page.

Ask the staff for a machine induction on your first visit if any seat or pin setting looks confusing.

About the shaking

Trembling under load is really common when you're new to lifting or coming back after a long break. Your nervous system hasn't yet learned to fire the muscle smoothly, and the small stabilising muscles tire before the big ones do. It tends to settle within a few weeks of regular training.

Treat it as information. Once a set starts to wobble, that's your last rep at that weight today. Slow lowering builds control faster than piling on weight does.

About the cracking

Painless pops in ankles and knees are usually gas bubbles releasing in the joint fluid, or a tendon flicking over a bit of bone. On their own they're harmless, and they often quieten once the muscles around the joint get stronger.

Step-ups, calf raises and the ankle drills in the desk reset aim right at those two joints.

See your GP if the shaking happens at rest or with very light weights, or comes with dizziness, numbness or pain. Same if a joint starts to hurt, swell, lock or give way. If you've never mentioned the shaking to a doctor, a quick check before you start lifting heavier in week 9 is a good idea.

Feed the work

A fruit smoothie on its own is mostly carbohydrate. Blend in Greek yoghurt, kefir, milk or a scoop of protein powder and breakfast becomes a third protein meal, which helps a lot when you're slim and trying to build muscle.

On gym days, eat something with protein within a couple of hours of finishing. Drink water through the session too; mild dehydration makes the shakes worse.

General guidance for a healthy adult, not medical advice. Stop any exercise that causes sharp pain.
